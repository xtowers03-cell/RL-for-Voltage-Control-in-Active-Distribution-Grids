\section{Classical, Optimization-Based, and DVPP Voltage Regulation}
Traditional Volt-VAR control in distribution grids relies on OLTCs, voltage regulators, and switched capacitor banks~\cite{Moutis2018,Satsangi2022}. With high inverter penetration, smart inverter standards such as IEEE~Std.~1547-2018~\cite{IEEE1547_2018} introduce local piecewise-linear Volt-VAR droop curves ($Q(V)$) capped at $\pm 0.44\,S_{\text{rated}}$~\cite{Satsangi2022}. However, fixed local droop settings underutilize capacity inside deadbands and fail to coordinate neighboring units once saturation occurs~\cite{Ortmann2020,Satsangi2022}.

To coordinate multiple distributed resources, Online Feedback Optimization (OFO) and distributed dual-ascent controllers exchange Lagrange multipliers between electrical neighbors~\cite{Ortmann2020}, forming the basis of the distributed controller (\texttt{D\_V}) evaluated alongside centralized least-squares (\texttt{LS\_V}) and recursive least-squares (\texttt{RLS\_V}) baselines in~\cite{Mughni2026}. At the system level, the DVPP framework~\cite{Marinescu2022,Haberle2023,Schulte2026} coordinates heterogeneous inverter-based resources under mutual active/reactive reserves.

\section{Reinforcement Learning and Safe MARL in Distribution Grids}
Survey literature highlights policy-based actor-critic methods under CTDE as particularly suited to continuous Volt-VAR control~\cite{Chen2022}. During offline training, a centralized critic exploits global nodal voltages—including unmeasured passive buses—while online actors execute from local observations~\cite{Chen2022}.

To guarantee constraint satisfaction and training stability, recent studies combine RL with graph-based neighborhood aggregation~\cite{Jacob2024}, communication-enabled adaptive safety filters (\texttt{CE-ASF})~\cite{Chan2026}, and two-stage curriculum learning with behavior cloning (BC) warm-starts~\cite{Wang2026}.

\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The high penetration of inverter-based distributed energy resources (DERs) in distribution networks frequently induces bidirectional power flows and nodal voltage violations, challenging traditional regulation mechanisms. Mechanical tap changers cannot track fast renewable fluctuations, purely local droop curves lack network-wide coordination, and centralized optimization relies on accurate grid models and extensive communication. To address these limitations, this thesis investigates reinforcement learning (RL) methods for decentralized supervisory voltage regulation on a modified IEEE 13-node test feeder with four photovoltaic inverters.

Assuming stable inner converter control loops, the RL controller operates at the supervisory layer to dispatch reactive power setpoints, using active power curtailment only as a last resort while respecting inverter apparent power limits. Under the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm, a centralized critic accesses all active and passive bus states offline, while decentralized agents operate online using only local and neighbor measurements. A composite reward penalizing voltage violations and operational effort keeps all network buses within statutory voltage limits (0.95--1.05 p.u.).

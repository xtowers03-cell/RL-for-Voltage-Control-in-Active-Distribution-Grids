\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The rapid proliferation of distributed energy resources (DERs), particularly renewables-based energy sources such as wind and solar photovoltaic (PV) systems, in active distribution networks frequently induces bidirectional power flows and nodal voltage violations, challenging traditional regulation mechanisms such as manual tap-changing transformers, purely local droop curves, and communication-heavy centralized optimization. This thesis investigates reinforcement learning (RL) methods for decentralized supervisory voltage regulation on a modified IEEE 13-node feeder distribution grid integrated with four distributed PV systems.

Assuming internally stable inner converter control loops, the RL controller operates strictly at the supervisory layer, determining reactive power setpoints (with active power curtailment used only as a last resort) while enforcing each inverter's circular apparent power rating by construction via normalized action scaling. Under the Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm, a centralized critic observes both active inverter buses and unmeasured passive load buses during offline training using a composite voltage-violation and operational-cost reward, whereas online execution relies solely on normalized local and immediate neighboring node measurements to keep all active and passive buses within statutory voltage limits (0.95--1.05~p.u.).

The proposed decentralized RL architecture is systematically evaluated across policy network depths (from linear and shallow models to multi-layer and graph-based architectures) and benchmarked against a centralized RL baseline, standard IEEE~1547-2018 Volt-VAR droop, optimization-based and distributed feedback controllers, and an exact AC optimal power flow (AC-OPF) upper bound under load, voltage, and generation disturbances.
